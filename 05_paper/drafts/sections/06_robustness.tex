\section{Robustness and Falsification}
\label{sec:robustness}

The current robustness story is a donor-pool sensitivity story. The older clean-core
specification produced a larger and borderline-significant negative effect; the food-cereal
rerun produces a smaller, cleaner, but statistically insignificant aggregate estimate.
That shift is not a nuisance detail. It is the main robustness result.

\subsection{Donor-pool sensitivity}

The clean-core donor pool was castor, guarseed413, cotton, jeera and turmeric. It was
useful for stress-testing the inherited positive-volatility claim, but it was not a
satisfactory counterfactual for food staples. Adding available food-cereal donors
(barley, maize, jowar and bajra) changes the DiD from the earlier clean-core $-20.7\%$
to the current $-9.8\%$, with CR1 $p=0.145$ and wild-bootstrap $p=0.153$.

The interpretation is straightforward: the inherited positive claim does not survive,
but neither does the stronger aggregate ``volatility fell by about 20\%'' claim. The
current paper should lead with the donor sensitivity and with the chana-specific signal.

\subsection{Placebo and pre-trends}

The current fake-ban placebo is $-6.5\%$ and insignificant (analytic $p=0.344$;
wild-bootstrap $p=0.433$). The joint pre-trend test does not reject ($p=0.445$).
Compared with the earlier clean-core run, the falsification battery is cleaner.

This cleaner falsification profile comes with the smaller main estimate. That is exactly
the kind of tradeoff a referee will expect to see reported rather than hidden.

\subsection{Leave-one-commodity-out}

Leave-one-out results reinforce the commodity-level reading. The aggregate negative
effect weakens when chana is removed (ex-chana $-7.4\%$, CR1 $p=0.294$), while dropping
wheat produces a stronger and significant result (ex-wheat $-15.2\%$, CR1 $p=0.003$).
Dropping mustard, soybean or moong leaves negative but insignificant estimates around
$-8.5$ to $-9.3\%$.

The conclusion is not that all treated commodities moved together. It is that the
aggregate decline is chana-centered, and that wheat should not be treated as a clean
source of identification.

\subsection{MSP and bundled-policy confounding}

Paddy remains dropped because its spot price is MSP-censored. Wheat remains flagged:
under the current food-donor Synthetic DiD it is essentially zero, and its post-2021
spot behavior is heavily contaminated by MSP/procurement, export restrictions, open-market
sales and stock limits. Chana remains the cleanest commodity through mid-2024, before
its own stock-limit interventions enter.

This is also a compound-treatment setting. The derivatives suspension arrives alongside
commodity-specific trade, tariff, stock-limit and procurement interventions. The current
result is therefore descriptive/quasi-causal, not a clean treatment effect of futures
suspension alone.

\subsection{Pending robustness}

The food-donor pull is not complete. Barley, maize and jowar are complete at district
level; bajra is partial; ragi, groundnut, sesamum and sunflower are not yet in the
district panel. The oilseed donors are especially important for mustard and soybean.
The next publishable rerun should finish that pull, rebuild the panel, rerun all C1
estimators, and then add Honest-DiD and staggered-DiD robustness.

\subsection{Summary}

The robust statement is now narrower and more credible: the suspension did not raise
spot volatility; the aggregate estimate is negative but not significant under the
current food-donor counterfactual; chana shows the strongest lower-volatility signal;
wheat is confounded; and the full donor/inference upgrade remains pending.
