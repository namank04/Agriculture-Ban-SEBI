\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the current C1 estimates after the food-cereal donor rerun. The
central empirical conclusion is narrower than the earlier clean-core draft: the inherited
claim that the suspension raised spot volatility by $8$--$10\%$ is refuted, but the
aggregate estimate is now modest and not statistically robust. The strongest lower-volatility
signal is concentrated in chana, while wheat no longer supports the headline once a food-staple
counterfactual is used.

\subsection{Current aggregate result: negative, but not significant}
\label{sec:results-aggregate}

The current district-panel two-way fixed-effects DiD estimate is
$\hat\beta=-0.103$, equivalent to a $-9.8\%$ effect on spot realized volatility. With
14 commodity clusters after adding food-cereal donors, the conventional clustered
$p$-value is $0.121$; the more relevant few-cluster CR1 $t$ reference gives
$p=0.145$, and the null-imposed wild-cluster bootstrap gives $p=0.153$. The aggregate
effect is therefore negative in sign but not statistically significant.

This is a material revision from the earlier clean-core result of $-20.7\%$
using only industrial/spice donors. The change is substantively important: once the
counterfactual is moved closer to food staples by adding barley, maize, jowar and
bajra, the estimated aggregate decline roughly halves and loses significance. The
paper's honest headline is therefore donor sensitivity, not a settled aggregate
causal effect.

\subsection{Where the lower-volatility signal survives: chana}
\label{sec:results-chana}

Synthetic DiD and synthetic control point to chana as the cleanest remaining
lower-volatility case. In the current output, per-commodity Synthetic DiD estimates
chana at $-38.7\%$ with a placebo-based $z=-2.05$. Commodity-level synthetic control
estimates chana at $-37.3\%$, with the in-space placebo at the resolution floor
($p=0.100$ with nine donors). This is the only treated commodity that looks strong
across the current estimator family.

The other treated commodities are weaker. Mustard is negative but not significant in
Synthetic DiD ($-15.2\%$, $z=-0.68$), soybean is negative but imprecise ($-26.1\%$,
$z=-1.32$), and moong is smaller and noisy ($-11.5\%$, $z=-0.53$). Wheat collapses to
approximately zero under Synthetic DiD ($+0.4\%$), consistent with the project-wide
concern that wheat is dominated by MSP, export-ban and stock-limit confounding rather
than the futures suspension itself.

\subsection{Falsification improves, but the main effect weakens}
\label{sec:results-falsification}

The current falsification battery is cleaner than the earlier clean-core run. The fake
December-2019 placebo returns $-6.5\%$ with analytic $p=0.344$ and wild-bootstrap
$p=0.433$. The joint pre-trend lead test gives statistic $2.67$ with $p=0.445$. These
are no longer warning signs in the way the earlier placebo and recent lead bin were.

The tradeoff is that the main aggregate effect is no longer significant. That is the
right interpretation of the rerun: improving the counterfactual and the falsification
profile reduces the headline estimate. The result should be framed as ``the suspension
did not raise spot volatility; lower-volatility evidence is concentrated in chana''
rather than ``the suspension robustly reduced volatility.''

\subsection{Conditional volatility (C2): no independent support for elevated volatility}
\label{sec:results-c2}

The ICSS-then-within-regime GARCH exercise remains unchanged in interpretation. Under
the HAC-corrected Sanso--Arago--Carrion detector, no commodity has an unconditional
variance break within $\pm 30$ trading days of its suspension date. The only detected
district-median break is in chana on 2018-09-14, well before the 2021 suspension.

The district-median chana GARCH fit remains partly degenerate, with persistence at the
IGARCH boundary in the long regime. We therefore do not headline a GARCH persistence
number. C2 provides no support for the inherited ``elevated volatility'' claim, and the
volatility evidence rests on the C1 panel and synthetic-control results.

\paragraph{Basis.}
The inherited post-ban basis-widening arm remains internally impossible as stated:
banned commodities have no exchange-traded futures after the suspension, so a post-ban
spot--futures basis cannot be constructed. That arm is deferred to the domestic-spot
versus international-futures insulation track.
